The Counter Utility Matrix

DATA           = HEROES ∪ MAPS ∪ META
for each domain D in { HEROES, MAPS, META }:
  INDEPENDENT(D) = ⋃ facts(s)      over each selection s in D
  DEPENDENT(D)   = ⋃ facts(s ⋈ t)  over the other selections t
  FACTS(D)       = INDEPENDENT(D) ∪ DEPENDENT(D)
FACTS          = FACTS(HEROES) ∪ FACTS(MAPS) ∪ FACTS(META)
FACTS(D) ∩ FACTS(E) = the joins of D with E
STRATEGIES     = CONSTRAINTS ∪ HEURISTICS ∪ ASSUMPTIONS
COMP           = ARGMAX[ STRATEGIES( FACTS ) ]

DATA: the tables as pulled. HEROES: kits, their published numbers, playstyles, counters, synergies. MAPS: mode, stages, sides, and the terrain the wiki's articles describe (chokes, interiors, high ground, flanks, sightlines, open ground, hazards, cover: mentions per thousand words). META: win, pick and ban rates per hero and per map, dated.

FACTS: what the fact engine derives for one board. The independent facts of a domain come from one row: a hero's pool, range and rates; a map's mode, stages and terrain (each feature z-scored across the maps with text); the meta's vintage. The dependent facts are joins (⋈): a hero's rate on this map (heroes ⋈ map_meta ⋈ maps), the style a map rewards (the rates' lift + the terrain's lean. Lift, playstyle ⋈ map_meta: a style's heroes' win rate there over their own, z-scored across the maps. Lean: brawl from chokes and interiors, dive from high ground, flanks and hazards, poke from sightlines and open ground, averaged, z-scored across the maps with text), who it answers (heroes ⋈ counters ⋈ heroes), who it pairs with, the six aggregated, the twelve compared, a ban against both teams' counters. Facts are numbered F1, F2, ...; every claim on the board cites one.

STRATEGIES: the playbook, one markdown file each. A constraint is a hard limit, or a bonus or penalty under a condition. A heuristic is a metric, a direction and a weight. An assumption is prose: shown, never scored.

COMP: of every legal six, the one the function scores highest on this board. Deterministic: the same board gives the same six.

The function: STRATEGIES( FACTS )

One six in, one number out. The facts are its namespace: team, enemy, matchup, map, world - the facts tab's own numbers.

A hard limit that fails removes the six; a soft one subtracts its penalty. A heuristic normalises its metric to 0..1 against 1,200 random legal sixes for the board (seeded from map, side, enemies and bans), flips it when minimising, and multiplies by its weight. A heuristic guarded on the six's own state (team.*, or a matchup.* blue decides) is a need: met in full it costs nothing, unmet it costs its weight, so entering the guarded state never pays. A scored constraint adds bonus minus penalty while its when holds. An assumption adds nothing.

for one six x, with every hard limit holding:

score(x) = Σ heuristics h     w_h · norm_h( metric_h(x) )
         + Σ needs n          s_n · w_n · ( norm_n( metric_n(x) ) − 1 )
         + Σ scored r         w_r · ( bonus_r(x) − penalty_r(x) )
         − Σ soft limits l    penalty_l(x)

norm_h(v) = clamp( (v − min_ref) / (max_ref − min_ref), 0, 1 )
            and 1 − that when h minimises
            min_ref, max_ref: over the reference sixes h's guard holds on
            max_ref = min_ref: a reward reads 0.5, a need costs nothing
s_n       = min( 1, 2 / Σ w over the needs on n's guard ): one state costs 2 at most

What 100 means. Each seat's best six on this board is its optimal, 100. An optimal at or below zero has no share to give: every other comp reads unscored. The badge above a picker is that seat's picks as a share of its optimal under this playbook, not a win probability. A seat with nothing to score against reads unscored; the reason is on hover.

Fight odds. Blue's share over the sum of both shares; red's is the rest. With one seat unscored the bars show the shares alone. Not a fitted probability.

The argmax. Each role's pool is the six heroes of highest standing: a hero's mean score across the reference sixes it is in, plus 0.5 per locked partner. Every legal shape is filled around the locked picks, each six is scored, and a local search swaps same-role heroes while the score rises, from the best six sixes and the best six of every shape within 4 points of the top; then it brings the wiki's synergy pairs into the best sixes two slots at a time. Ties break by mean map win rate, then by name.

Reach. The weights make a hero common or rare, never impossible: every released hero is in the optimal six on some board that suits it, within a match's five bans.

When nothing scores: every legal six is zero, the board reads unscored, and the optimal is the tie-break alone.

The board

Red's most likely starting comp: a two-two-two, filled slot by slot with the likeliest hero from the map's pick rates and the synergy table, past the bans. No strategy is read. It does not change when red reveals a pick.

likelihood(h) = pick(h, map) + 2 × partners(h, the six so far)

pick(h, map)   the hero's pick rate on this map (overall with no map set)
partners(h, S) heroes already on the six the synergy table pairs h with

Blue's optimal counter to current picks: solved against red's revealed picks, or their likely comp until they reveal one, on this map, side and bans; never against blue's own picks. The suggestion tiles are a second solve that keeps your locked picks. Red's optimal is solved as the scale for red's score and not shown.

The weights. A heuristic's weight is the slider under its card. It stays in your browser and rides with every request until reset; the file's weight is the default. Store writes it to the file through the tune tool, logged. Only a heuristic's weight has a slider, 0 to 10; a scored constraint keeps its file's.

How the pieces fit

sources → db/data (fetch) → db/psql (the database) → ui/facts (FACTS of a board)
                                                                    ↓
                   inference/strategies (STRATEGIES) → inference/solver (ARGMAX) → the board

The data layer (db/): the sources in one Postgres schema; the MCP tools are the one door. A sentry watches the strategy files, the database's free text and the audit log.

The inference layer (inference/): the playbook and the solver. Expressions compiled once and whitelisted; no model, no network.

The board (ui/): the facts of one board and the inference layer's answer at each stage of a draft. It never calls a language model.

Claude: runs when you ask. Refreshes the sources, derives a strategy's mathematics from its prose, tunes weights. /comp is the same solver in chat.
