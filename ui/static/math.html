The Counter Utility Matrix

Countrix is short for Counter Utility Matrix, the equation below. The counter is who answers whom. The utility is what a pick is worth once the facts are counted. The matrix is every selection crossed with every other: heroes against heroes, heroes against maps, both against the meta. A comp is the argmax over that matrix.

DATA           = HEROES ∪ MAPS ∪ META
for each domain D in { HEROES, MAPS, META }:
  INDEPENDENT(D) = ⋃ facts(s)      over each selection s in D
  DEPENDENT(D)   = ⋃ facts(s ⋈ t)  over the other selections t
  FACTS(D)       = INDEPENDENT(D) ∪ DEPENDENT(D)
FACTS          = FACTS(HEROES) ∪ FACTS(MAPS) ∪ FACTS(META)
FACTS(D) ∩ FACTS(E) = the joins of D with E
STRATEGIES     = CONSTRAINTS ∪ HEURISTICS ∪ ASSUMPTIONS
COMP           = ARGMAX[ STRATEGIES( FACTS ) ]

DATA: the tables as pulled. HEROES: kits, their published numbers, playstyles, counters, synergies. MAPS: mode, stages, sides, and the terrain the wiki's articles describe, counted as mentions per thousand words (chokes, interiors, high ground, flanks, sightlines, open ground, hazards, cover). META: win, pick and ban rates per hero and per map, dated.

FACTS: what the fact engine derives for one board. The independent facts of a domain come from one row: a hero's pool, range and rates; a map's mode, stages and terrain, each feature z-scored across the maps with text; the meta's vintage.

The dependent facts are joins (⋈). A hero's rate on this map is heroes ⋈ map_meta ⋈ maps. The style a map rewards adds the rates' lift to the terrain's lean: lift is playstyle ⋈ map_meta, a style's heroes' win rate there over their own, z-scored across the maps; lean reads brawl from chokes and interiors, dive from high ground, flanks and hazards, poke from sightlines and open ground, then averages and z-scores across the maps with text. The rest are who a hero answers (heroes ⋈ counters ⋈ heroes), who it pairs with, the six aggregated, the twelve compared, and a ban weighed against both teams' counters. Facts are numbered F1, F2, …; every claim on the board cites one.

STRATEGIES: the playbook, one markdown file each. A constraint is a hard limit, or a bonus or penalty under a condition. A heuristic is a metric, a direction and a weight. An assumption is prose: shown, never scored.

COMP: of every legal six, the one the function scores highest on this board. The same board gives the same six.

The function: STRATEGIES( FACTS )

One six in, one number out. The facts are its namespace: team, enemy, matchup, map and world, the facts tab's own numbers.

The default engine scores every six before the playbook reads it, whatever the playbook holds: the six's win rates on this map, the wiki's synergy pairs among its picks, and the counter graph between it and the other side. A pick's win rate is read as its edge over a coin flip, trusted by its pick rate, so a hero picked in few matches counts for less. The counter graph is the wiki's edges, its Match-Up tables' and its Strategy sections', and where the wiki has no edge either way, the answers the kit derives: thirteen mechanisms read off the two heroes' kits, from hitscan against a flier to anti-heal against self-healing, each loser's %(TOP_ANSWERS)d best at a score of %(THRESHOLD)g or more. A wiki edge counts %(WIKI_WEIGHT)d, a derived one %(DERIVED_WEIGHT)d, and the board names every derived edge it counts with the mechanism that fired. The other side is its locked picks, or its likely six on this map until it locks one; only this term reads the likely six or a derived edge - the playbook's counter metrics read the wiki's graph alone. A playbook of assumptions alone gets the six these three favour, scored and explained, and every other term sits on top of them.

A hard limit that fails removes the six; a soft one subtracts its penalty. A heuristic normalises its metric to 0..1 against %(REFERENCE_SIZE)s random legal sixes for the board (seeded from map, side, enemies and bans), flips it when minimising, then multiplies by its weight. A heuristic guarded on the six's own state (team.* or matchup.*) is a need: met in full it costs nothing, unmet it costs its weight, so entering the guarded state never pays. A scored constraint adds bonus minus penalty while its when holds. An assumption adds nothing.

for one six x, with every hard limit holding:

score(x) = base(x)
         + Σ heuristics h     w_h · norm_h( metric_h(x) )
         + Σ needs n          s_n · w_n · ( norm_n( metric_n(x) ) − 1 )
         + Σ scored r         w_r · ( bonus_r(x) − penalty_r(x) )
         − Σ soft limits l    penalty_l(x)

base(x)     = %(W_RATE)g · rates(x) + %(W_SYNERGY)g · synergy(x) + %(W_COUNTER)g · counters(x)
rates(x)    = mean over the picks p of  t_p · ( win_p − 50 )
              t_p = pick_p / ( pick_p + %(RATE_PICK_HALF)g )
              win_p, pick_p: p's rates on the map, all ranks; overall with no map or no row
synergy(x)  = the wiki's synergy scores, summed over the pairs in x
counters(x) = Σ over x's picks p and the other side's heroes e of  w(e, p) − w(p, e)
              w(l, a) = %(WIKI_WEIGHT)d where the wiki reads a answering l, 0 where it reads the
              reverse, else %(DERIVED_WEIGHT)d for a derived answer, else 0
              the other side: its locked picks, else its likely six here

norm_h(v) = clamp( (v − min_ref) / (max_ref − min_ref), 0, 1 )
            and 1 − that when h minimises
            min_ref, max_ref: over the reference sixes h's guard holds on
            max_ref = min_ref: a reward reads 0.5, a need costs nothing
s_n       = min( 1, %(NEED_BUDGET)g / Σ w over the needs on n's guard ): one state costs %(NEED_BUDGET)g at most

The base's weights. The rate term is in win-rate points and weighs %(W_RATE)g. The synergy and counter weights are set so that, on the median board, each term spreads the reference sixes about half as far as the rate term does; the calibration is in inference/base.py, and recorded matches are to refit it. A heuristic moves a six by its weight at most, since its norm is 0..1, and a base term has no such bound. On the median board a heuristic at weight 1 moves a six less than any base term spreads the sixes, at 3 about as far as the synergy or counter term, and at the slider's 10 further than the three together.

What 100 means. Each seat's best six on this board is its optimal, 100. The badge above a picker is that seat's picks as a share of its optimal under the default engine and this playbook, not a win probability; the rate term's zero is a coin flip, so a share is of the optimal's edge over one. An optimal at or below zero leaves no share to divide, so every other comp reads unscored, and the reason is on hover.

Fight odds. Blue's share over the sum of both shares; red's is the rest. A seat still drafting is read through its fill, the best six its picks can reach, on both sides alike: a sum over fewer picks reads low, so the seat with more picks would lead. With one seat unscored the bars show the shares alone. This is not a fitted probability.

The argmax. Each role's pool is the six heroes of highest standing: a hero's mean score across the reference sixes it appears in, plus %(PARTNER_POINTS)g for each locked partner. Every legal shape is filled around the locked picks and scored. A local search then swaps same-role heroes while the score rises, starting from the best %(SEEDS)d sixes and from the best six of every shape within %(SHAPE_REACH)g points of the top, and finally brings the wiki's synergy pairs into the leading sixes two slots at a time. Ties break by mean map win rate, then by name.

What is searched, and what is not. There are 18,040,386 legal sixes. The search scores about 14,500 of them, near enough 0.08%%, so the answer is not a proof that nothing better exists. Two things cut the space. The pool keeps each role's six highest-standing heroes, a truncation that can drop a hero who is weak alone and decisive beside one partner. The local search reaches back out of the pool: it swaps against the whole released roster, restarts %(RESTARTS)d times inside the leading shape, and changes two seats at once, which is how a pair that only pays together is found. The suite checks the reaching back on every run: on small boards of a twelve-hero test roster under a two-two-two role queue, each role's pool cut to half, the search returns the maximum a full enumeration finds, with the default engine on and off, and on at least one of them the winning six holds a hero the pool cut. The tests page has the larger proofs.

What it is optimal against. A comp is optimal against the red you can see, not against every red they might pick. Before red reveals anything the board answers their most likely starting comp, drawn from the map's pick rates; each pick they show re-solves against what is now known. It is a best reply to present information and changes as that information does. The hedge is the if countered optimally case the board tool reports: it scores your six against red's best reply to it, so a comp that wins only while red plays badly shows up there. The board page does not solve it.

Reach. The weights are meant to make a hero common or rare, never impossible. What is established is narrower: under the shipped playbook, where the default engine alone scores, 44 of the 53 released heroes have a board on which they are in the optimal six, within a match's five bans, and each such board is a proof. The search found none for nine: Domina, Emre, Freja, Hazard, Kiriko, Shion, Sierra, Sojourn and Venture. That is not evidence that no such board exists, only that a search over a few maps and a few reds did not find one. No rule forbids any of them; the engine does not value what they do on the boards it tried, and the playbook's rules are what can answer that.

When nothing scores - the default engine off, as a caller can turn it, and no term of the playbook scoring - every legal six ties at zero, the board reads unscored, and the optimal is the tie-break alone.

The board

Red's most likely starting comp: a two-two-two, filled slot by slot with the likeliest hero from the map's pick rates and the synergy table, past the bans. No strategy is read, and it does not change when red reveals a pick.

likelihood(h) = pick(h, map) + %(SYNERGY_PULL)g × partners(h, the six so far)

pick(h, map)   the hero's pick rate on this map (overall with no map set)
partners(h, S) heroes already on the six the synergy table pairs h with

Blue's optimal counter: solved against red's revealed picks, or their likely comp until they reveal one, on this map, side and bans; never against blue's own picks. Once blue picks, its seat shows blue's six above the optimal: from one to five picks, the picks with the rest filled by a second solve that keeps them - the six the suggestion tiles offer; at six, the six itself. The game plan describes that six. Red's optimal is solved as the scale for red's score and not shown.

The weights. A heuristic's weight is the slider under its card. It stays in your browser and rides with every request until reset; the file's weight is the default. Store writes it into the file through the tune tool, and logs it. Only a heuristic has a slider, 0 to 10; a scored constraint keeps the weight in its file.

How the pieces fit

sources → db/data (fetch) → db/psql (the database) → facts/ (FACTS of a board)
                                                                  ↓
                   inference/strategies (STRATEGIES) → inference/solver (ARGMAX) → the board

The data layer (db/): the sources in one Postgres schema.

The facts layer (facts/): the World, the metrics registry and the FactSet - every table turned into numbered facts, each metric defined once for the board and the solver alike.

The inference layer (inference/): the playbook and the solver. Expressions are compiled once and whitelisted. The solver runs no model and opens no connection; serve.py reads the World per request, and derive.py asks the claude CLI for a draft's frontmatter.

The door (door/): the MCP tools, the one way in for a write, and the sentry over the strategy files, the database's free text and the audit log.

The board (ui/): the facts layer's facts of one board and the inference layer's answer at each stage of a draft. It never calls a language model.

Claude: runs when you ask. It refreshes the sources, derives a strategy's mathematics from its prose, and tunes weights. /comp is the same solver in chat.
