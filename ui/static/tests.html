What is claimed

One thing, precisely: the six this board returns is the highest-scoring six the playbook allows — the argmax of the function the math page sets out, over every legal team.

That is a claim about the search, not about the game. Whether a higher score wins more matches is a separate question, and one this app cannot currently answer: no outcomes are recorded anywhere, so nothing here is fitted or validated against a match being won. The checks below establish that the solver optimises its own objective. They establish nothing about whether that objective is right.

Three kinds of check

There are about 9.6 × 1021 distinct boards — a map, a side, any red of nought to six, any bans of nought to five, any locks. No sample covers that, so three different checks are run, each answering what the others cannot.

checkwhat it establisheswhat it cannot
designed 100 boardsno systematic bug: every input dial is varied on purpose, so a flaw in ban handling or lock seating has somewhere to appearnothing about a rate — it is not a sample of anything
random 200 boardsa bounded failure rate: n clean out of n, drawn uniformly, puts the rate under 3/n at 95% confidencea rare structured bug a uniform draw seldom reaches
adversarial 399 boardsa counterexample, if one exists — worth more than any number of confirmationsabsence; and a miss the oracle also makes stays invisible

The designed proof

Every legal six is enumerated and scored, and the true maximum compared with what the solver returns. A board with no bans has 18,040,386 legal sixes across all eighteen role shapes; bans cut that.

candidates scored  1,352,230,508

The figure that matters most is not the hundred. On 37% of these boards the winning six held a hero the per-role pool had cut, and the local search reached back out of the pool and found it. Without the restarts and the two-at-once swap the solver would return a beaten comp on about one board in three.

The adversarial hunt

Enumerating a board costs minutes, so it is not how to search widely. A stronger searcher is the oracle instead: the same objective over a field seven times wider. Where the shipped solver and the oracle agree, the board passes; where they differ the oracle has found something better, and that board is escalated to a real enumeration.

The boards are drawn to be awkward on purpose — bans that evict the heroes a board would otherwise lean on, locks on weak picks, reds of every size.

boards   399
misses     0

This is the check that was expected to find something. A search over seven times the field found nothing the shipped search had missed.

The random sample

The designed set varies each dial deliberately, which is how a bug is found and not how a rate is estimated. This draws boards uniformly instead — any map, any side, a red of nought to six, bans of nought to five, locks of nought to three — so the result bounds how often the solver is wrong.

By the rule of three, 200 clean boards puts the failure rate under 1.5% at 95% confidence. A thousand would put it under 0.3%.

The regression gate

Proving a board costs a full enumeration, which is far too slow to run on every change. So the expensive part is done once and the answer recorded: tests/fixtures/optimal.json holds, per board, the six a brute force found and what it scored. The suite re-solves each and checks the solver still reaches it. The enumerations the fixture is cut from are hours of compute and live outside this repository; scripts/optimal.py reads them from the paths in OPTIMAL_SOURCES and rewrites the fixture.

The fixture also records the digest of the playbook its boards were proved under. While the shipped playbook scores nothing, every legal six ties, there is no maximum to reach and the gate skips; under any other playbook that scores, it fails with recorded under a different playbook. Boards with bans are held out until they are enumerated again, so the gate says nothing yet about the search under bans, although the three checks above all draw boards with bans.

That is what stops a later change quietly undoing this — a narrowed pool, a dropped two-swap, a broken constraint shows up as a board that used to be exact and is not.

Beyond the boards, the properties the engine has to hold whatever it is asked:

the parallel and single-process paths agree bit for bita worker split must not decide an answer

A hard limit is enforced with nothing rewarding it; an extreme heuristic drives the pick to its metric's true optimum over the whole legal space; the two compose. Each was checked against a playbook holding that one rule, where the right answer can be worked out without the solver.

What is not proven

  • That the objective is right. The checks show the solver maximises its own score. Nothing shows a higher score wins more games, because no outcomes are recorded. The weights are a judgement, not a fit.
  • That the answer is optimal against every red. It is a best reply to the red you can see, and it changes as red reveals. The if countered optimally row is the hedge.
  • That no board fails. A hundred designed and a few hundred sampled boards are a vanishing fraction of the space. What is established is a bounded rate and no systematic bug, which is not the same as none.
  • That every hero is pickable. Fifty-one of the fifty-three released heroes have a board found for them, each a proof they seat. Two have none found - which is not a proof that none exists. The reach search is a limited one, and its silence is not a theorem.
